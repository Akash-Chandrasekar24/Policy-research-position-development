# Stakeholder Position Analysis

A policy position that ignores who it affects and who can block or amplify
it is incomplete. This skill produces a structured map of stakeholders,
not a generic list.

## Candidate stakeholder categories

Only include categories genuinely relevant to the specific question —
don't pad the table:

- Union Government (which ministry/department)
- State Government (Karnataka, where relevant)
- Citizens / constituents (Bangalore South specifically, where relevant)
- Startups and companies (which segment — e.g. early-stage AI startups)
- Large companies / industry bodies
- Regulators (which one)
- Researchers / academic institutions
- Workers (which sector)
- Civil-society organisations (which ones, and on what grounds)

## Method

1. For each relevant stakeholder, determine their **position**: support,
   oppose, conditional support, or genuinely unknown (state "unknown" if
   you cannot find a stated or reasonably inferable position — do not
   invent one).
2. Determine their **main concern** — the specific thing driving their
   position (compliance cost, public safety, market access, jobs).
3. Determine their **influence**: High / Medium / Low, justified by
   something concrete (lobbying track record, electoral weight, media
   reach, institutional veto power) — not a guess.
4. Ground every position/concern in a source — a stated position, past
   testimony, or published statement, with its literal URL — or mark it as
   a reasoned inference and say so explicitly instead of citing a URL that
   doesn't exist.
5. Flag stakeholders whose interests directly conflict — this is exactly
   the tension the trade-off analysis and position drafting need to see.
6. Where a stakeholder is a Bangalore South-based body (a local industry
   association, RWA, civic group), say so explicitly — a constituency MP's
   office needs to know which stakeholders are local versus national.

## Output format

Table: `Stakeholder | Position | Main concern | Influence (H/M/L) | Bangalore South-based? (Y/N) | Source URL or "reasoned inference"`

## Common failure modes to avoid

- Fabricating a stakeholder's position because it seems plausible.
- Treating "industry" as one undifferentiated stakeholder when startups
  and large incumbents often want opposite things.
- Rating influence by intuition rather than a stated justification.
- Writing a source name ("per a news report") instead of the literal URL.
