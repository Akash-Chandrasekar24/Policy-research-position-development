# Legal and Institutional Feasibility

An option can look good on paper and still be practically impossible to
enact or enforce. This skill checks that specifically.

## Method

For each option, assess two distinct questions:

1. **Legal feasibility**
   - Does enacting this require new primary legislation, a rule/notification
     under existing law, or executive action alone? Each has a different
     timeline and different political requirements.
   - Does it conflict with existing law (Centre-State jurisdiction under the
     Constitution's lists, existing sectoral statutes, international
     commitments)?
   - Is there a plausible constitutional or judicial-review challenge?

2. **Administrative feasibility**
   - Does an institution with the mandate and capacity to implement this
     already exist, or would a new body be needed?
   - Does the relevant regulator have the technical capacity (staffing,
     expertise) this option assumes?
   - Is there a track record of this type of institution succeeding or
     failing at similar mandates in India?

Cite the literal source URL for any specific law, article, or notification
referenced (e.g. the Constitution list entry, the Act's official text, a
regulator's mandate page) — naming a law by title alone is not a citation.

## Output format

```
Option: <name>
Legal feasibility: High/Medium/Low — <reasoning: what law/process is needed (source URL)>
Administrative feasibility: High/Medium/Low — <reasoning: which institution, what capacity gap (source URL)>
```

## Common failure modes to avoid

- Assuming administrative capacity exists because the mandate exists on
  paper — check for a track record, not just a job description.
- Treating "this would need a new regulator" as a minor implementation
  detail rather than a multi-year feasibility risk.
- Skipping the Centre-State jurisdiction question for options that touch
  areas Karnataka also regulates.
- Citing a law or article number without a link to verify it.
